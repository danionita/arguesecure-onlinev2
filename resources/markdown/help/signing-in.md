# Signing in

You sign in with your email and password. Your administrator creates the
account, unless your administrator has switched on sign-up, in which case the sign-in page has a **Create one** link.

If your administrator has set up email, **Forgot password?** on the sign-in page sends you a one-time link (good for an hour) to choose a new one. The page says the same thing whether or not the address has an account. Using the link signs you out everywhere; then sign in as usual. Otherwise, ask an administrator to reset it.

## Creating an account yourself

If **Create one** appears on the sign-in page, enter your email address. You
get a message with a link; open it, press the confirm button and choose your
password. Until then the account has no password. The page says the same thing
whether or not the address already has an account. Your name starts as the part
of your address before the @; change it in your profile. If you follow the link
but do not choose a password, use **Forgot password?** on the sign-in page. Your
administrator may allow only certain email domains.

## Single sign-on

If your organisation has set it up, the sign-in page has a **Sign in with …**
link. It takes you to your organisation's sign-in and back. The first time,
it connects to your account by your email address (or creates one for you, if
your administrator allows it for your organisation). A new account starts with
no access: you see only public assessments until someone shares one with you.

If your administrator has turned on *Sign in with SSO only*, use that link;
passwords work for administrators only. Changing your email then asks you to
sign in again at your organisation first.

## Two-factor authentication

You can add a second step: a six-digit code from an authenticator app on
your phone. Anyone who learns your password still cannot sign in without
your phone.

**Turn it on** from your **Profile**, under *Two-factor authentication*:

1. Enter your password and choose **Set up**.
2. Scan the QR code with an authenticator app, or type the key into it.
3. Enter the six-digit code the app shows. Nothing is turned on until you do.
4. Save the **ten recovery codes** shown next. They appear once.

**Signing in** then takes two steps: your password, then a code. If you tick
*Remember me*, it applies once the code is accepted (administrator accounts are never remembered). Each code works once,
so wait for the next one if a code was just used.

**Lost your phone?** Type one of your recovery codes in place of the code.
Each recovery code works once. Your profile shows how many are left, and you
can make a new set with your password and a code (the old ones stop working). If you have none
left, ask an administrator to remove your second factor; you then sign in
with your password and set it up again.

**Turn it off** from your Profile with your password and a code. Your other
sessions are signed out when you do.

Your administrator may require two-factor authentication. If so, you are
asked to set it up straight after your password, before you can use
anything else.

## Emails from the application

If your administrator has set up email, you may get a message when someone
mentions you, assigns you a node, or a summary draft you asked for is ready.
**Your Profile** lists the ones your administrator allows, under *Email me
when*, and you can turn each off; every email also ends with a link to stop it.
The same events always appear in the application. Emails gather for ten
minutes and go out as one, with a link and no comment text.
